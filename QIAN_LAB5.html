Recent prospects jump higher.

Question: Do today's prospects jump higher than those of twenty years ago?

Max vertical

Yes—on the max vertical.

Across the 350 early-era prospects and 299 recent-era prospects with a recorded result, the recent group averaged 35.50 inches versus 33.20 inches in the early group—a 2.30-inch difference. The two-sided Welch t-test gives a p-value less than .001.

0.64 SD Cohen's d · a meaningful shift for a max-vertical screen

The signal, in inches.

Both charts use a zero-based vertical scale. That keeps the distance honest: the reader can see the level and the change without a cropped axis.

Recommendation

Raise the max-vertical benchmark for first-pass screening—but keep it as one input, not a total athleticism score.

The 0.64-standard-deviation shift is large enough to change a max-vertical screen. It is not large enough to make a final scouting decision by itself, especially when the project question is deliberately kept to this one measure.

Limitation

This result cannot rule out a different pattern on the other combine measures. The t-test shows that max vertical changed; it does not show that prospects are more athletic on every measure.

Method note: code used to compute the yearly chart from Clean

The year series was computed from the workbook's Clean sheet by grouping numeric LPVERT values by YEAR. Text NA values were excluded. The comparison values and test result were checked from Benchmarks, Lab5_Checks, and python3 run_tests.py.

from collections import defaultdict
from openpyxl import load_workbook

workbook = load_workbook("QIAN_LAB4.xlsx", read_only=True, data_only=True)
sheet = workbook["Clean"]
headers = {value: index for index, value in enumerate(next(sheet.iter_rows(values_only=True)))}
by_year = defaultdict(list)
for row in sheet.iter_rows(min_row=2, values_only=True):
    year = row[headers["YEAR"]]
    lpvert = row[headers["LPVERT"]]
    if isinstance(year, (int, float)) and isinstance(lpvert, (int, float)):
        by_year[int(year)].append(float(lpvert))

for year in sorted(by_year):
    values = by_year[year]
    print(f"{year}: n={len(values)}, mean_LPVERT={sum(values) / len(values):.2f} inches")
